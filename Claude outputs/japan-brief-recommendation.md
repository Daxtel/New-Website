## Three things your input changed

### 1. The seven days is a promise, not a wait

You are right that it is a delivery window, not effort. But do not present it as
"we might be busy, so allow a week." Present it as a commitment:

> **Your brief is delivered within seven days of our call.**

Same fact, opposite read. As a delay it sounds like a constraint you are apologising
for. As a guarantee it sounds like a studio that ships on schedule, which is what
you are selling. It also silently sets the sequence: no call, no clock.

Do not put "7 days" anywhere before the call is booked. The form should not promise
a turnaround for a call that has not happened yet.

### 2. The call-commitment question is the most important field in the form

You nearly forgot it and it is doing more work than anything else on the page. It
does three jobs at once: it qualifies (anyone unwilling to take a call is not a
buyer), it sets the sequence, and it is a micro-commitment that raises the show-up
rate.

Make it **required**, not optional. Suggested wording:

> **The brief starts with a conversation.**
> To give you something specific rather than generic, we need 45 minutes to
> understand your goals, your category, and what you have already committed to.
> Your brief is delivered within seven days of that call.
>
> ☐ Yes, I can make 45 minutes for that call

No tickbox, no brief. That is not a barrier, it is the filter.

### 3. Price it from day one, and give it away

This is the change I would push hardest for.

Your instinct is right that free now, paid later is the way to validate. The risk is
that free becomes permanent by default, and that raising a price later reads as a
price increase, which people resist.

Instead, put the price on the page from the first day and waive it:

> **The Japan Market Brief**
> Normally ¥150,000. Free for the first ten.

Three things happen at once. The value is anchored before they read a word about
what is in it. Scarcity does real work, which matters because you genuinely cannot
do many of these. And when you switch to paid, nothing changes except a line coming
off the page. No announcement, no awkwardness, no price rise. The price was always
there.

Pick the number now even if you are unsure. You can adjust before the tenth one.

### The switch trigger, decided in advance

Write this down now or free becomes permanent:

> The brief becomes paid when **either** ten have been delivered, **or** I am
> turning away more than I accept.

Whichever comes first.

## The form

Keep it short. Every extra field costs completions, and the call does the real
qualifying.

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Name | |
| Work email | |
| Company | |
| Company website | you will look at it before the call anyway |
| Stage (the 5 you already have) | routes the brief and segments the mailing list |
| Category / what you sell | one line, free text |
| What is the main thing you want to get right? | one line. this is the best pre-call intel on the form |
| ☐ Yes, I can make 45 minutes for that call | **required** |
| ☐ Send me the monthly Japan market note | **separate, unticked, optional** |

That last one stays separate from everything else. Booking a call is not consent to
marketing email, and a Japan-entry audience will include EU and UK companies.
One unticked box, stored with a timestamp.

## Gating by stage

| Stage | What they get | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Exploring | Guide + monthly note | Not committed to Japan. A free week of your time on someone still deciding is the definition of unqualified. They re-enter when they commit. |
| 2. Planning | **Brief** | Ideal. Decided, building strategy, nothing locked that you would have to unwind. |
| 3. Preparing | **Brief** | Ideal. The creative audit lands hardest here. |
| 4. Launched | **Brief** | Strong. Live assets to audit, a real performance problem. |
| 5. Scaling | Straight to a call | Past diagnosis. Book them, do not brief them. |

## Build order

The quiz is the biggest piece and the least urgent. Do not let it block the rest.

1. **The CTA block in the article.** Needs nothing but a confirmed name and the
   form URL. Half a day.
2. **The form.** Reuse the restaurant quiz pattern that already exists. Feed it to
   HubSpot as a contact with the stage as a property, not just an email to admin@.
3. **The brief template.** The five-part document, built once, so each one is
   assembly rather than authoring. This is what makes six hours realistic.
4. **The quiz.** 60 questions with per-answer feedback, bilingual. Its own project.
   Ship steps 1 to 3 first and let the form do the qualifying until the quiz exists.
