# The free diagnostic: naming and structure

**Status: proposal. Nothing here is published until Jackson confirms it.**
Everything below is a design suggestion, not a description of an existing service.

---

## 1. The name

Current working name: "7-day Japan market review"

Three problems with it. "Review" is passive and does not say what gets reviewed.
"7-day" leads with your constraint rather than their gain. And "free review" reads
as a commodity audit, which is what every agency offers.

### Recommended: **Japan Market Brief**

Why it works:

- "Brief" is native to your industry. A creative brief, a production brief. It
  signals that a studio is doing this, not a consultancy, which is your actual
  differentiator.
- It names the deliverable. They get a brief. Concrete beats abstract.
- It is what the document genuinely is: the thing a real engagement would start
  from. You are giving away the first artefact of the work.
- Short, premium, no duration in it.

In copy: "The Japan Market Brief. Delivered in seven days. No cost."

### Alternates

| Name | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Japan Readiness Review** | Laddering directly off the quiz's readiness score | "Review" still passive; overlaps quiz language, which could confuse or could reinforce |
| **Japan Blind Spot Brief** | Direct tie to the quiz headline you already wrote | Strong hook, slightly gimmicky for a premium buyer |
| **Japan Entry Diagnostic** | Sounds rigorous and clinical | Consultancy language, moves you away from studio positioning |

My order: Japan Market Brief, then Japan Readiness Review.

## 2. What happens in the seven days

Designed so the work is genuinely deliverable by a small studio (roughly six to ten
hours of actual labour across a seven-day window, not seven days of effort), and so
that every element uses something you know that the client cannot get from a
consultancy.

### Days 1 to 2 — Intake

- One 45-minute call
- They send: brand guidelines, current global creative, any existing Japan
  materials, target category and price band
- You establish: what they think their positioning is, what they have already
  committed to, what stage they are actually at

### Days 3 to 5 — The work

Four passes, each one mapping to a section of the pillar article, which means the
article does the pre-selling for this.

**Reference set mapping.** Who they will actually be compared to in Japan. Not
their global competitors. The domestic brands occupying their shelf, price band
and category. Foreign brands cannot do this for themselves, and it is usually the
single most surprising page in the document.

**Positioning read.** Which parts of their promise travel and which proofs have to
be rebuilt from local material.

**Creative audit.** The differentiated part. Their existing assets assessed against
Japanese market conventions: register, pacing, density, format, casting. Specific
and frame-level, not general. A consultancy hands them a market report. You hand
them "here is why this film will not land here, and here is what changes."

**Format and channel reality check.** What the work actually has to be, and where.
Vertical-first requirements, platform conventions, what their current asset library
is missing.

### Days 6 to 7 — Delivery

- The written brief
- One 30-minute readback call
- Ends with a recommended first move and a scope

---

## 3. What they receive

A document with five parts:

1. **Reference set** — the brands you will actually be measured against in Japan
2. **Positioning read** — what travels, what has to be rebuilt
3. **Creative audit** — specific findings on your current assets
4. **Format and channel priorities** — what the work has to be
5. **Recommended first move, with scope** — what to do, and what it takes

Part five is the conversion mechanism, and it is honest. The document ends with
what to do next. Doing it is what you sell.

## 4. The funnel logic

The quiz and the brief are one ladder, not two offers.

```
Guide (/blog/japan-market-entry-guide-2026)
   ↓  reader wants to know where they stand
Quiz (5 stages, 12 questions, readiness score + blind spots)
   ↓  qualified stages only
Japan Market Brief (free, 7 days, written document)
   ↓  ends with a scope
Engagement
```

### Who should NOT get the brief

This is the part that makes it sustainable. Suggested gating by quiz stage:

| Stage | Offer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Exploring | Guide + newsletter | Not committed to Japan yet. A free week of work on someone still deciding whether to enter is the definition of an unqualified lead. |
| 2. Planning | **Brief** | Ideal. Decided to enter, building strategy, has not locked decisions you would have to unwind. |
| 3. Preparing | **Brief** | Ideal. Actively building, and the creative audit lands hardest here. |
| 4. Launched | **Brief** | Strong. They have live assets to audit and a performance problem to solve. |
| 5. Scaling | Direct to call | Past diagnosis. Book them, do not brief them. |

Stage 1 should still convert into something. The guide plus the monthly email is the
right landing place, and they re-enter the funnel when they commit.

## 5. Open questions for Jackson

1. **Is the labour estimate right?** Six to ten hours across seven days. If the
   creative audit alone takes longer, either narrow it (one asset, not the library)
   or make the brief paid.
2. **Cap the volume.** Free plus human work needs a ceiling. Two or three briefs a
   month is a sane limit, and scarcity helps the pitch: "we take three of these a
   month."
3. **Does the gating above match how you actually want to spend your time?**
4. **Quiz scope.** Five stages times twelve questions is sixty questions with
   per-answer feedback. That is a substantial content build in itself, and it needs
   to be right in both languages. Worth scoping separately from the CTA.
