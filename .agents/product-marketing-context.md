## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** International brands entering Japan consistently underperform because they apply global creative playbooks without cultural adaptation, hire generalist agencies that treat Japan as "just another APAC market," or rely on translation instead of true localization.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Global agencies treat Japan as a checkbox in APAC campaigns — no deep local knowledge
- Local Japanese agencies often lack bilingual communication and struggle with international stakeholder management
- Freelance translators can translate words but can't adapt positioning, tone, or visual language for cultural fit
- In-house teams lack Japan-specific production infrastructure and vendor relationships
**What it costs them:** Failed launches, wasted media spend, damaged brand perception in a market where first impressions are extremely durable, and months of lost revenue while they recalibrate.
**Emotional tension:** Fear of getting Japan wrong — luxury brands know a bad Japan launch can permanently damage their positioning in one of the world's most discerning consumer markets.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Large global agencies with Tokyo offices (Dentsu International, McCann Tokyo) — fall short because they're expensive, slow, internally siloed, and treat premium brands as small fish within massive portfolios.
**Secondary:** Boutique Japan-based production companies — fall short because they lack strategic capability, international stakeholder communication skills, and the bilingual project management premium brands need.
**Indirect:** DIY approach (brand's internal team + freelance translators + local vendors) — falls short because it fragments accountability, produces inconsistent quality, and requires the brand to project-manage across language and cultural barriers.
